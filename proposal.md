# Labour Policy Proposal

## Executive Summary

Artificial intelligence is not producing aggregate job losses in Australia. It is producing a recomposition of employment that aggregate indicators are poorly equipped to detect.

Exposure research — ILO Working Paper 140 (2025) and the OECD AI Capability Gap index (2026) — identify transformation rather than displacement as the dominant mechanism. DEWR (2026) finds no economy-wide disruption and reports employment among 20-24 year olds as more resilient than among those aged 25 and over since late 2022. This need not rule out contraction at entry level in exposed occupations. If such occupations account for only a modest share of youth employment, their contraction would be difficult to detect in an age-based aggregate — particularly alongside derived demand from AI infrastructure, with 13,000 of the 72,000 additional electricians and technicians required by 2030 linked directly to data centre construction. Aggregate resilience may be a signature of recomposition rather than evidence against it.

Compositional problems require targeted instruments. Yet the 2026-27 Budget withdraws $266.2 million over four years from employer apprenticeship incentives. This proposal recommends redirecting that expenditure rather than creating new programs.
